# AI Tells — English

Use this as a diagnostic reference, not a banned-word dictionary. A single item is not proof of AI writing. Look for clusters, repetition, and context.

## 1. Structural tells
- Repeated "thesis → explanation → takeaway" paragraphs.
- Every section has the same length and internal shape.
- Excessive headings for short content.
- Introductory paragraphs that restate the prompt.
- Conclusions that merely summarize the article instead of adding a useful final judgment.
- Symmetrical lists with three or four perfectly balanced items.

## 2. Formulaic transitions
Watch for repeated or unnecessary use of:
- Moreover, furthermore, additionally
- It is worth noting that
- It is important to note that
- In this context
- Against this backdrop
- Taken together
- Overall, it is clear that
- This highlights the importance of
- This underscores the need to
- In conclusion

Do not remove a transition when it carries real logical meaning.

## 3. Lexical tells
Commonly overused AI-flavored vocabulary:
- robust
- comprehensive
- nuanced
- multifaceted
- transformative
- groundbreaking
- pivotal
- compelling
- increasingly important
- landscape
- paradigm
- delve into
- shed light on
- foster
- leverage
- facilitate
- underscore
- empower
- unlock

Prefer concrete verbs and nouns when the source supports them.

## 4. Syntactic tells
- Excessive parallel constructions.
- Repeated "not only... but also..." structures.
- Repeated three-part rhetorical lists.
- Long sentences with several subordinate clauses when a short sentence would be clearer.
- Uniform sentence length.
- Overuse of passive voice without a reason.
- Parenthetical qualifications stacked into the same sentence.
- Every paragraph ending with a polished takeaway sentence.

## 5. Rhetorical tells
- Generic enthusiasm without evidence.
- Excessive hedging that avoids making any useful judgment.
- False balance when evidence is asymmetric.
- Manufactured urgency.
- Generic empathy that sounds interchangeable across topics.
- "The key takeaway is..." followed by something already obvious.
- Significance inflation: a preliminary finding is described as transformative.

## 6. Formatting tells
- Excessive em dashes.
- Bolded phrases every few lines.
- Colon-heavy sentence fragments.
- Too many bullets where prose would be more natural.
- Decorative headings such as "The Bottom Line" used repeatedly.

## 7. Voice tells
- No identifiable preference or judgment.
- Excessive politeness.
- Constantly explaining what the reader should think.
- Vocabulary that is more sophisticated than the author's apparent context.
- No concrete details, examples, or friction points.

## 8. Humanization rules
1. Remove patterns, not words in isolation.
2. Replace abstractions with concrete language only when the source supports it.
3. Preserve uncertainty and evidence level.
4. Preserve the author's stance rather than inventing a stronger one.
5. Allow asymmetry, short sentences, fragments, and ordinary wording when appropriate.
6. Do not insert fake anecdotes, personal experience, or invented citations.
7. Never optimize for an AI detector at the expense of meaning, accuracy, or voice.
